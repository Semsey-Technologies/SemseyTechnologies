Problem Solving Quotient (PSQ)

Measuring Applied Adaptive Intelligence

The Problem Solver Score

How to measure how good you are at fixing things in real life.

The PSQ is a novel framework designed to measure an individual's adaptive and applied problem-solving ability in real-world contexts. Unlike traditional IQ tests, PSQ prioritizes dynamic thinking, strategic execution, and constraint navigation.

The PSQ is a new way to see how "smart" you are at solving real-life problems. Traditional IQ tests only look at how you do on paper, but the PSQ looks at how you handle tough situations, limited time, and big challenges.

The Six Core Pillars The 6 Skills You Need

PSQ evaluates performance across six critical dimensions of practical intelligence:

We look at 6 different ways you can be good at solving problems:

01

Problem Identification Finding the Root

Detecting and clarifying the root issue rather than reacting to symptoms. Figuring out what's *actually* broken instead of just fixing the small stuff.

02

Strategic Thinking Planning the Fix

Creating structured, goal-oriented approaches to complex challenges. Making a smart, step-by-step plan to get things done.

03

Creative Innovation New Ideas

Generating novel solutions within strict operational or technical constraints. Coming up with cool, new ways to solve a problem when you don't have many options.

04

Constraint Navigation Limited Resources

Operating effectively with limited time, resources, or available options. Knowing how to get the job done even when you're short on time or money.

05

Implementation Doing the Work

Executing and adapting plans in real-time as new data emerges. Actually putting your plan into action and changing it if things don't go as expected.

06

Collaborative Intelligence Teamwork

Coordinating problem-solving efforts with others effectively. Working well with other people to find the best solution together.

Sample scenarios used to evaluate the pillars through judgment and foresight.

Strategic Thinking The Plan

Challenge: You're launching a new product with almost no money and very little time.

"What is your plan? What do you do first, and how do you know it's working?"

Constraint Navigation Doing More with Less

Challenge: A major bug must be fixed in an hour, but you can only change three lines of code.

"How do you decide what to fix so that everything stays stable?"

Scoring & Evaluation Criteria How we Grade You

  • Clarity of Thought: Clear Thinking: How well is the root issue understood? Do you actually understand what's broken?
  • Logic and Flow: Systematic: Is the reasoning coherent and systematic? Does your plan make sense from start to finish?
  • Feasibility: Practical: Are proposals realistic and actionable? Is your plan something that can actually be done?
